# Vela Night Care: service guide

Vela Night Care is an after-hours home-visit medical service for international visitors and expats in Medellín, Colombia. A Vela intake assistant answers by phone, browser call or Telegram, checks safety, takes the details and, when a visit is right, sends a general practitioner to where the caller is staying. This is a demo service: the insurer, plans, prices and doctors are fictional.

## Hours
- The Vela line answers 24 hours a day. It is built for the night, when clinics are closed, and most calls arrive between 8:00 p.m. and 7:00 a.m., Medellín time (America/Bogota).
- Emergencies are always 123, at any hour.

## Where we visit
Medellín and the south of the Aburrá Valley: El Poblado (including Provenza and Manila), Laureles, Estadio, Belén, Centro, Robledo, Envigado and Sabaneta. Other nearby areas are handled case by case; the doctor's arrival time may be longer.

## How a visit works
1. The assistant first checks for emergency warning signs. If any are present, the caller is told to call 123 and given the nearest emergency department. Vela never sends a home visit for an emergency.
2. The assistant verifies travel insurance, asks where the caller is and whether they are alone, and takes the symptoms and every medication taken in the last 24 hours.
3. The assistant checks each medication against Colombia's INVIMA registry and US openFDA labels, and warns if two products contain the same active ingredient.
4. A triage level is set. Urgent visits target arrival within 2 hours. Standard visits target arrival within 6 hours. Mild, stable cases are offered a morning visit.
5. The nearest available doctor receives a briefing in Spanish and confirms on their phone. The caller hears the doctor's name, the arrival time and a case number, and receives a confirmation email in English.

## What the doctor does
- A general practitioner examines the patient at home, explains what they find and recommends treatment.
- Only the doctor can diagnose, prescribe or advise on doses. The Vela assistant never does.
- If the doctor decides the patient needs a hospital, they help arrange the transfer.

## Language
- The assistant speaks English. The doctor receives a full briefing in Spanish, so the caller does not have to repeat everything.
- Most Vela doctors speak some English. The caller can also keep the Telegram chat open to help with translation.

## Cost and insurance
- Members of Andes Assist plans with home visits (Traveler Basic, Traveler Plus, Expat Annual) pay nothing at the door. Vela bills the insurer directly.
- If coverage cannot be verified, the visit still goes ahead. Care is never delayed over insurance. The self-pay price for a night visit is COP 280,000 (about USD 70), paid to the doctor by card or bank transfer, and Vela provides an invoice to claim back from the insurer.
- Medicines the doctor prescribes are bought separately at a pharmacy (droguería). Many in El Poblado and Laureles deliver at night.

## Getting ready for the doctor
- Keep every medicine taken in the last 24 hours in one place, with the boxes, so the doctor can see them.
- Have a passport or ID and the insurance details ready.
- If the building has a porter (portería), tell them a doctor from Vela is coming. Unlock the door if it is safe to do so.
- Turn on a light and keep the phone nearby. The doctor will call or message on arrival.
- The doctor will give their name and the Vela case number when they arrive. If they cannot, do not let them in and contact Vela.

## Changing or cancelling a visit
- To cancel, message the Vela Telegram chat or call back with the case number. There is no charge for cancelling before the doctor arrives.
- If symptoms get worse while waiting, call 123 straight away. Do not wait for the doctor.

## Privacy
Vela shares the caller's details only with the doctor assigned to the visit and, for billing, with their insurer. Conversations are recorded for quality and safety review.
