# Emergencies in Medellín: a guide for visitors

## The number to call
- 123 is Colombia's single emergency number for ambulance, police and fire. It works from any phone, including foreign mobiles, and is free.
- The operator usually answers in Spanish. Speak slowly, give the address first, then say what is happening.
- If the caller cannot speak Spanish, they can say "No hablo español, ¿alguien habla inglés?" (No AH-bloh es-pahn-YOL, ahl-GYEN AH-bla een-GLES?). If nobody does, the phrases below are enough to get an ambulance sent.

## When to call 123 instead of waiting for a home visit
Call 123 straight away for: chest pain or pressure; trouble breathing; signs of a stroke (face drooping, arm weakness, slurred speech); fainting or not waking up properly; sudden confusion; a seizure; heavy bleeding that will not stop; swelling of the lips, tongue or throat; vomiting blood or passing black stools; a severe headache with a stiff neck; thoughts of self-harm. Also call 123 for a suspected poisoning or overdose.

## Phrases for the 123 call
- "Necesito una ambulancia." (I need an ambulance.)
- "La dirección es..." (The address is...) followed by the street address, building name and apartment number.
- "Estoy en el barrio El Poblado, en Medellín." (I am in the El Poblado neighborhood, in Medellín.)
- "Tiene dolor en el pecho." (He or she has chest pain.) / "Tengo dolor en el pecho." (I have chest pain.)
- "No puede respirar bien." (He or she cannot breathe properly.)
- "Se desmayó." (He or she fainted.)
- "Está convulsionando." (He or she is having a seizure.)
- "Está sangrando mucho." (He or she is bleeding a lot.)
- "Es alérgico y se le está hinchando la garganta." (He is allergic and his throat is swelling.)
- "Mi número de teléfono es..." (My phone number is...)

## While waiting for the ambulance
- Stay on the line with 123 and follow their instructions.
- Unlock the door and tell the building porter (portería) that an ambulance is coming: "Viene una ambulancia para el apartamento..." (An ambulance is coming for apartment...).
- Do not eat or drink unless the operator says so. Sit or lie in the position that feels easiest to breathe.
- If someone is with you, ask them to wait at the entrance to guide the paramedics.

## Emergency departments (urgencias) in the Medellín area
The nearest emergency department is looked up live for each caller from OpenStreetMap, and that result always takes priority over this list. These hospitals and clinics have 24-hour emergency departments and are well known to taxi drivers:
- Clínica Medellín, El Poblado
- Clínica Las Vegas, El Poblado
- Clínica El Rosario, sede El Tesoro, El Poblado
- Clínica Las Américas, Belén and Laureles area
- Hospital General de Medellín, near Centro
- Hospital Pablo Tobón Uribe, Robledo and Estadio area

To go by taxi, say: "Lléveme a urgencias de la Clínica..." (Take me to the emergency department of the... clinic). In a true emergency an ambulance through 123 is safer than a taxi.
